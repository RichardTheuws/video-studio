# Extended — the longer video (45–90 s), with an AI voice and subtitles

> A form for explainers, tips and social-proof videos that need more than plates can carry. Brand teasers and promos stay
> short; a product spotlight can go either way. The first production in this form was a 66-second report explainer (2026-11-17).

## 1. What Extended is (and isn't)

- **Length 45–90 s including the end block.** 90 s is a hard cap (`qa-check.sh --length 45-90`). Shorter than 45 s = a normal video.
- **More content, not slower.** Longer may never be "filler": every beat carries a new fact or a new step. The rhythm
  discipline from `commercial-craft.md` §7 and `directing.md` §6 still holds, with the explainer limit (mean shot < 4.0 s;
  spread > 0.6 s). A beat that has to stay longer gets motion *inside* the recording (a real scroll, a frame that lights up),
  never a Ken Burns.
- **The voice carries the story, the subtitles make it readable without sound.** At 10–30 s the plates carry everything; above
  45 s that no longer works without turning into a wall of text. So: AI voice + subtitles, and plates only as **chapters**.
- **Always 16:9 and 9:16**, each its own master (`vertical-916.md`): screen recordings at the target format, never a crop.

## 1b. Approval before the voice

Order, also for your own brands: **script + storyboard → `story-check.py --phase storyboard` PASS → the person who owns the
brief approves the storyboard → only then `gen.py`.** Record the approval in the brief (`approved: "<who>, <date>, <channel>"`).
A fork, subagent or unattended run without that person in the loop stops after the storyboard and delivers it as files
(script per line, shot list, the story-check result). For a client order, the client approves the board. Why: an explainer
that was produced without an approved storyboard had to be redone, and a script that needed a voice fix needed a new script
and storyboard anyway — recording the voice first wastes every step after it.

## 2. Structure

| Part | What | Target length |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | The peak or the promise in the first 1.5 s (scenario.md S-A01) | 4–6 s |
| Chapters | 3–6 chapters, 1–3 beats each; one thought per VO line | 8–15 s per chapter |
| Call | One action (scan, order, call) | 5–7 s |
| End block | Brand end card + tail (the brand's existing block) | 6–8 s |

- **Script:** 110–200 words at ~2.2 words/s (measured for Chatterbox Dutch on a cloned voice: 2.16–2.3 w/s; measure your own
  voice and language). Write the **intended text** per line (= the subtitle) in `lines.json` and derive the **speech text**
  from it with fixed spellings (`ChatGPT → tsjet-gee-pee-tee` and `AI → ee-aai` for a Dutch voice, brand names spelled the
  way they sound, domains written out with the spoken dot). The subtitle stays correctly spelled while the voice says it right.
- **Chapter plate:** a `plate` without `pos`, short ("02 · Where you stand") with `bron`, only in the first ~2.5 s of the
  chapter (reading-time gate), then it goes and the picture is free. Never `pos: "onder"` together with subtitles (they collide).
- **Shot lengths come from the measured VO durations**, not from a table: read the WAV duration again on every build.
  Recordings are made after the VO is fixed, with the holds on those durations.

## 3. Voice and sound

- **AI voice, always disclosed.** On screen and in the metadata comment (EU AI Act art. 50 covers synthetic audio). The
  chain clones a **reference voice** with Chatterbox multilingual (MIT). **No voice ships with this skill** — bring a
  reference you have the rights to: a recording of a person who consented in writing to voice cloning for this use, or the
  output of a TTS model whose licence allows that use and redistribution. Check the licence of the reference, not just of the
  engine: a voice generated by a model under a use-restricted licence (e.g. RAIL-M) carries those restrictions along. Put it at
  `assets/voice/anchor.wav` (git-ignored) or point `VO_ANCHOR` at it, compute its sha256 and add it to `reference/voices.json`
  after a listening test (that list is the gate, see §5).
- **Never let the pipeline pick a voice by itself.** A multilingual model with an English-speaking stock voice gave a Dutch
  script a heavy accent, and whisper's language ID did not notice. The voice list in `voices.json` is the accent gate.
- **A real human voice** instead of AI is an exception, not a default: only with that person's consent for that production,
  listed under `real_voices` with `only_for` the production folder(s), and credited as a real recording.
- **Takes:** 3 seeds per line, picked by the gate in `scripts/vo/measure.py`: WER 0 with the vocab prompt, every brand name
  heard without the vocab hint by two whisper models, f0 within the reference, no sound island after the last silence. A human
  ear decides when in doubt. Don't open a line with the brand name if you can avoid it: a brand name as the first word of a
  sentence got clipped in 6 of 7 takes.
- **Pronunciation:** loanwords and brand names go through `reference/lang/<lang>/pronunciation.json` before recording, and
  `scripts/vo/loanwords.py <workdir>` refuses a script with a listed loanword that has no pronunciation (`gen.py` runs it
  itself). Both lists are per language; `nl` ships as a worked example, copy its shape for your language. Whisper writes a
  mispronounced word correctly, so only these lists (and a human ear) catch it.
- **Clean clips:** `pick.py` cuts every take on the voice bounds (Chatterbox often adds noise or a thud after the sentence),
  removes clicks and normalises linearly; `voice-check.py` judges tail, edge, dynamics and sample rate per clip
  (`scripts/vo/clean.py`).
- **Bed:** an AI music bed first through `adeclick,lowpass=f=4500` (2×), judged with `qa-check.sh --bed` (clicks).
- **Mix:** VO at −16 LUFS, the bed −20 dB below it with a sidechain duck, loudnorm I=−14 (social) on the master.
- **Label:** one EU label (`commercial-craft.md` §0.6). If the brand's end block already carries it, no second `ailabel`; then
  name the voice early in text, e.g. in the source line of the first chapter plate ("… · voice made with AI"). If the end block
  has no label: `ailabel` on the end card. Always the metadata comment with the voice and its credit (`qa-check.sh` checks the
  AI marking, `voice-check.py --master` checks that the voice is named).

## 4. Subtitles

- **Source = the script, not recognition.** The text comes from `lines.json` (intended text). Timing: the VO line starts at a
  measured moment; within a line the cues follow mlx-whisper word times on that one WAV (word_timestamps), at the word
  boundary closest to the sentence break. Whisper only delivers **times**, never text.
- **Rules:** ≤ 2 lines, ≤ 42 characters per line, ≥ 1.0 s, ≤ 17 characters/s, no overlap, break at punctuation.
- **Two forms, always both:** burnt in via the title item `sub` (`templates/titles`, bottom centre, above the AI label and the
  app UI; `white-space: pre`, so only your line breaks — 42 characters fit in both formats; the layout gate catches overflow
  and collisions) and a sidecar `.srt` per format for web/YouTube. ffmpeg without libass is no problem: burning in runs
  through HyperFrames, like the rest of the title layer.
- **Placement: a free band, not over the content.** In the first production the screen recordings ran to the bottom, so the
  subtitle fell over numbers and source lines. Rule: the subtitle sits in a **fixed band** and the composition keeps it calm.
  - **16:9:** band = bottom 230 px (y 850–1080; `.landscape .sub { bottom: 120px }`). Frame screen recordings so there is
    **no number, source line, button or heading** in that band: scroll the capture a little higher or place the picture in a
    frame with ≥ 230 px margin at the bottom. What the voice talks about never sits in the band.
  - **9:16:** band = y 1380–1560 (`.portrait .sub { bottom: 420px }`), above the app UI (bottom ~360 px) and left of the button
    rail (`left/right: 60px` and ≤ 42 characters keep the right ~140 px free). The content sits above it (y 220–1360);
    chapter plates and the source line never in the band.
  - **Close-ups of software:** app text runs to the bottom. Lay a soft gradient into the app's paper colour under the band
    (close-ups only; wide shots keep their bottom), so nothing readable sits under the subtitle. Hide the app's own header when
    the film's brand mark sits top left (otherwise two logos).
  - Check by eye (§5): per chapter one frame *with* a subtitle; if something readable sits under it, the composition is wrong
    (don't move the subtitle: move the picture).
- **Gate:** `qa-check.sh <mp4> --srt <srt>` measures structure and sync (mlx-whisper on the final product, first word per cue
  within ±0.6 s, median ≤ 0.25 s, ≥ 80% found).

## 5. QA (on top of `commercial-craft.md` §7)

```bash
qa-check.sh exports/<name>-16x9.mp4 --length 45-90 --cuts work/film-16x9/cuts.txt --titles work/film-16x9/spec.json \
  --srt exports/<name>-16x9.srt --lufs -14 --bed audio/bed-as-mixed.wav
legibility-check.py work/film-16x9/spec.json exports/<name>-16x9.mp4 --cuts work/film-16x9/segments.txt --xfade 0.3
story-check.py --brief brief.md --phase final --titles work/film-16x9/spec.json --vo audio/vo/vo.json --frames strip.jpg
$VS_VO_PY voice-check.py audio/vo/vo.json --master exports/<name>-16x9.mp4
```
- **Two cut files.** `qa-check.sh --cuts` reads cut distances (shot lengths); `legibility-check.py --cuts` reads SEGMENT
  DURATIONS including the dissolve overlap (`t += duration − xfade`). Giving both the same file shifts every transition by
  k × xfade; write both from the build script. A film of mostly hard cuts: `--xfade 0`.
- `--lufs -14`, not `--social` on the HD master: `--social` also demands ≤ 16 MB, and a 70-second HD master is bigger (70.9 MB
  measured). The social export (CRF 25–26) is a separate step with `--social`.

Then the eye: a frame strip ≤ 0.7 s over the whole film, every cut out/in, and per chapter one frame with the subtitle on it
(readable on white and on dark footage, not over a plate's source line).

## 5b. The chain in scripts (generic, reusable per order)

Requirements (Apple silicon for mlx-whisper): a Python venv with `chatterbox-tts` (MIT), `mlx-whisper`, `librosa`, `scipy`,
`torch`/`torchaudio`, plus `ffmpeg` in PATH. Tell the scripts where it is with `VS_VO_PY`:
```bash
python3 -m venv ~/.venvs/video-studio-vo && ~/.venvs/video-studio-vo/bin/pip install chatterbox-tts mlx-whisper librosa scipy
export VS_VO_PY=~/.venvs/video-studio-vo/bin/python VO_LANG=en VO_ANCHOR=/path/to/your/reference.wav
S=<skill>/scripts/vo
$VS_VO_PY $S/gen.py     <workdir>      # lines.json → takes/<id>-s1..3.wav (loanword check first)
$VS_VO_PY $S/measure.py <workdir>      # → measure.jsonl (WER with vocab.txt, names without the hint, f0, mfcc, tail)
$VS_VO_PY $S/pick.py    <workdir>      # → vo/<id>.wav + vo.json (word times); exit 3 = a line fails the gate
python3   $S/subs.py <workdir>/vo.json starts.json work/<name>   # → .subs.json (title items) + .srt
```
Exit 3 from `pick.py`: first `gen.py <workdir> --seeds 6` and `measure.py` again; only then `pick.py --anyway` (the FAIL is
then recorded in vo.json and belongs in the QA report). `starts.json` comes from your timeline: VO line k starts after line
k−1 plus 0.42 s (within a chapter) or 0.72 s (new chapter).
- **`--seeds N` makes take sN for every line**, and `measure.py` measures them all, so `pick.py` may silently swap a take
  that was already approved. Redo one line in a workdir with only that line, pin approved takes (`pick.py <workdir> 04=04-s2.wav`),
  commit `vo.json` after approval and compare after every run.

## 6. Running it unattended (optional)

The chain is plain scripts, so a runner (CI job, queue worker, a headless agent) can drive it per order. What it needs:
1. A **length parameter** on the order (`standard | extended`; Extended only for the types in the intro).
2. **Script first:** the runner writes `lines.json` (110–200 words) and puts it in the storyboard for approval (§1b) — the
   text is approved before any voice is made. Without a human approval channel, the runner stops after the storyboard.
3. **Voice:** Chatterbox with the approved reference voice, 3 seeds, gate `measure.py` + `pick.py`.
4. **Recordings on the VO durations**, then the montage with `sub` items and the `.srt` from the same times.
5. **Gates:** `qa-check.sh --length 45-90 --srt …`, `legibility-check.py`, `story-check.py`, `voice-check.py`; mlx-whisper must
   be available to the runner's Python (`VS_VO_PY`).
